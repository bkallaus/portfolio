PRISM DUEL

An original two-player gem duel for the browser. Twenty-five tokens, sixty-seven cards, and three ways to win — built for two people to play together remotely.

React TypeScript WebRTC Vite
Play the game →

How to play

Same screen

Pass the laptop back and forth, or sit side by side. Nothing to set up.

Two devices

The game connects the two browsers directly to each other, peer to peer, with no account and no server in between.

On your own

An engine opponent plays the other side, so there is always a game waiting.

The shape of a turn

Twenty-five tokens fill a five-by-five board. On your turn you take up to three of them in one unbroken line — a row, a column or a diagonal, with no gap and no gold in the way. Or you take a gold token and reserve a card for later. Or you spend what you have and buy one.

Every card you buy discounts the next purchase in its own colour, so the early turns are about building an engine and the late ones are about spending it. Cards carry crowns and abilities too: steal a token, take another turn, claim a privilege that buys you a free gem on some future turn.

Three ways to win

  • 20 prestige points in total — the long game.
  • 10 prestige points on cards of a single colour — the monopoly.
  • 10 crowns — the rush.

Your opponent is racing all three at once, and the board only holds twenty-five tokens. Taking what you need often means handing over exactly what they were waiting for.

An original game

Prism Duel is its own game. The card costs, bonuses, crowns, abilities, names, palette and artwork were written for this project; nothing here is reproduced from a published one. What it shares with the tableau-building genre is its mechanics — drawing tokens from a grid, buying cards that discount later purchases, racing to a threshold — which is the part nobody owns.

The rules engine is pure and has no idea a browser exists, which is what lets a fuzz suite play six hundred random games against it and check, after every single step, that the tokens, cards and privileges all still add up. That suite found a real deadlock in an early draft — two players sitting on ten unspendable tokens, taking and discarding forever — and the fix is now a rule of the game.
